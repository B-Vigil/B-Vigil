Research

My interdisciplinary research develops theory and models to understand the mathematical properties and structure of complex systems.

I am particularly interested in interdisciplinary questions regarding biological processes expressing both continuous and network features. My research praxis fuses applied and computational algebraic topology, mathematical modeling, data science and interdisciplinary collaboration to target challenging problems from epilepsy and neurodegenerative disease to climate change and biodiversity.

I am also interested in geometric and topological representation learning. Below is a list of published and in progress papers.

Biography

Hello, and welcome! My name is Bradley; I'm a Quad Fellow and recent Ph.D. graduate in mathematics at Texas Tech University supervised by Dr. Travis Thompson. My research interests focus on Applied Algebraic Topology, Homotopy Theory, and Topological Data Analysis. I am currently interested in problems at the intersection of Applied Algebraic Topology and biological processes on complex networks as well as neuropathologies such as Alzheimer's disease. Furthermore, I have a keen interest in machine learning, in particular, I am interested in the applications and theory of Geometric and Topological Deep Learning.

To learn more about my research, please refer to the research section. You can also download my CV below to learn more about me!

Playground

Mathematics is easier to feel than to read. Below are a few things you can touch — small canvases that respond to your mouse. The first row runs from vector calculus to topology; the finale is a hands-on Čech nerve, the construction at the heart of how we turn a point cloud into a shape. Drag points, slide radii, and watch the structure appear.

Interactive Math

Hover, drag, and slide. Every card is live.

Dot Product & Projection

Drag either arrow tip. The shadow is the projection of b onto a; the readout is the dot product.

Gradient Ascent & Descent

Move over the landscape. The arrow follows ±∇f — uphill fastest for ascent, downhill fastest for descent — always perpendicular to the level set.

The Moving Frame

A frame rides the curve: tangent T, normal N, and the osculating circle of radius 1/κ. Move the mouse to steer it; pick a curve above.

Riemann Sums

Vietoris–Rips & β0

Drag any disk — grab it anywhere, not just the center — or double-click to add one. An edge appears when two disks meet; a triangle fills when all three edges are present.

H0 & H1 Persistence Barcode

Drag any disk or double-click to add one. H0 bars track components merging; H1 bars track loops that are born when a cycle of edges closes and die when triangles fill it in. Choose the triangle rule (Rips or Čech) — it changes when loops die.

The Čech Nerve

Cover a point cloud with disks of radius r. The nerve of that cover records its combinatorics: one vertex per disk, an edge when two disks overlap, and a filled triangle exactly when three disks share a common point. The Nerve Theorem says this simplicial complex captures the true topology of the union of disks. Drag the points, sweep the radius, and read the structure below (computed up to dimension 2 — vertices, edges, and triangles).

Tip: double-click empty space to add a point, or drag a point onto another to remove it. The “Čech rule” fills a triangle only when the three disks truly share a point (min-enclosing-circle radius ≤ r); switch it off for the looser Vietoris–Rips flag rule.
